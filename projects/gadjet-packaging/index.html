OVERVIEW

GadJet and Goodiz products span different categories, dimensions, price points and retail contexts, but still need to appear as part of one recognisable family. The packaging system was developed to maintain that consistency while giving each product enough flexibility to communicate its own form, features and technical information.

The packaging is designed for customers encountering the products across UK forecourt petrol stations, convenience stores, motorway service stations and Heathrow Airport click-and-collect, as well as through GadJetUK, Amazon and eBay. It therefore needs to communicate quickly in a physical retail environment while remaining clear and consistent when viewed as an online product listing.

As part of GadJet's Product Design and Graphics team, my role connects packaging layouts, product visualisation, iconography, labelled illustrations, multilingual manuals and print-production artwork across the Premium, G-Series and Essential ranges.

The problem

Customers often encounter these products in fast-moving retail environments where the packaging has limited time and space to explain the product. At the same time, the same design must withstand closer comparison through e-commerce imagery, where features, compatibility and technical information can be examined in more detail.

The challenge was to make two brands and three product ranges feel recognisably connected while accommodating products with different forms, price points, feature sets and packaging dimensions. The system also needed to carry consistently into product CGI, manuals, online listings and production artwork without treating every product as an unrelated one-off design.

One system, multiple ranges

The system gives each panel a clear purpose. Front panels prioritise the product, brand, compatibility and strongest selling points; side panels carry supporting features through repeatable icon sets; and rear or internal panels provide contextual imagery, specifications and mandatory information.

These shared roles create a recognisable family while allowing each range to respond differently. Premium packaging supports richer imagery, internal panels and specialist finishes, G-Series applies a more compact version of the hierarchy, and Essential packaging reduces it to the information needed for smaller value-led formats.

Packaging structure

Premium packaging is developed as a complete physical system rather than only an outer graphic. Standard formats create a more consistent shelf presence, with depth and structure adapting where the product requires additional space. The pack establishes the product identity and key information, the fitted internal tray holds and presents the product, and the accompanying manual carries the supporting instructions in a consistent format.

Together, these components extend the same hierarchy and visual language from shelf presentation through to unboxing and product use.

Product Imagery

Packaging artwork and layouts are created in Adobe Illustrator. Blender is used to produce packaging mockups, product renders and supporting imagery, with final adjustments completed in Adobe Photoshop. 

Lighting, materials and camera angles are chosen to keep the form and smaller details of each product clear at packaging scale. Product views are framed consistently to show the front and side together where useful, keeping ports, connectors, controls and branded details visible. Cable renders also separate overlapping layers so their construction remains legible at a small size.

The same imagery is adapted for packaging panels and GadJetUK product listings, maintaining a consistent representation of each product across physical and digital touchpoints.

Communicating the Product

Each pack needs to communicate its main benefits within a limited amount of space. Feature imagery, concise descriptions and iconography work together to make technical information easier to understand at a glance while maintaining a consistent hierarchy across the range. 

Front-of-pack icons combine a short performance value or product symbol with concise English and French descriptions. Category and feature icons follow consistent pairings, spacing and placement, while compatibility markers use controlled accent colours where products require differentiation. Cable length, charging output, audio and usability information can therefore be compared quickly without overwhelming the main product imagery.

Side-panel icons extend the same visual language across a wider range of technical and compatibility features, including charging standards, connectivity, product construction and device support.

Manuals and Localisation

Supporting manuals cover operating instructions, product care and safety information. Approved content is laid out in French, German, Dutch, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Turkish and Arabic while maintaining a consistent hierarchy across every language. Labelled illustrations identify important product components clearly without relying entirely on written instructions. 

Physical Finishes

Selected packaging uses foil, spot UV varnish, embossing and debossing to add contrast, reflection and tactility. Production artwork separates these treatments into clearly identified layers, giving manufacturing partners precise locations for each finish while protecting small product details and mandatory information. Close-up photography shows how the finishes respond to light and surface texture on the final manufactured packaging.

Outcome

The resulting system carries the same product story from the front of the pack through its internal tray, supporting manual and online listing. Shared layout rules, imagery standards and production conventions make the approach repeatable across different products while preserving clear distinctions between the Premium, G-Series and Essential ranges.

Across my time at GadJet, I have worked on more than 100 packaging assignments across the GadJet and Goodiz brands, typically producing the packaging, manual, shipping marks and fitted product tray for each product.

The packaging has been deployed across physical retail, owned e-commerce and major online marketplaces, including UK forecourts, convenience stores, motorway service stations, Heathrow Airport click-and-collect, GadJetUK, Amazon and eBay. This breadth demonstrates a system designed to remain recognisable and informative across substantially different purchasing contexts.

Manufactured packaging provides the final test of the system: CGI, typography, iconography, localisation and specialist finishes come together as one physical customer experience rather than a collection of separate design assets.
