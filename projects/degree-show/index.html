Following the conclusion of the Class of 2024 Graphic Design Degree Showcase, the exhibition was digitally reconstructed as a permanent virtual archive. The aim was to preserve not only the work on display, but the experience of moving through the space - allowing the show to be revisited after the physical exhibition had closed. Developed independently after the physical show closed, the finished environment combines LiDAR capture, photogrammetry, 3D modelling and real-time rendering. The exhibition was first reconstructed in Blender, then adapted for Unreal Editor for Fortnite and published as an explorable Fortnite island. 

Preserving the exhibition

The physical degree show brought together a year of student work across portfolios, posters, moving image, digital experiences and three-dimensional artefacts. Once the exhibition closed, that work could only be revisited through photographs and individual project records.

The virtual exhibition was created to preserve the show as a complete environment. Its layout, identity and displayed work remain connected, allowing previous visitors to return and giving those who could not attend an opportunity to experience it. 

Reconstructing the space

LiDAR scans and photographic references established the proportions and layout of the classroom. The scans provided a spatial base from which the architecture, furniture, display structures and recurring exhibition elements could be rebuilt more accurately in Blender. 

Posters and portfolio work were photographed and recreated as surfaces within the environment, while physical artefacts were captured using photogrammetry. Combining manual modelling with real-world capture made it possible to retain the individual character of the work while building a scene suitable for real-time exploration. 

Three-dimensional student work required a different form of documentation. Selected physical pieces were photographed from multiple angles and reconstructed through photogrammetry before being cleaned, textured and placed back into their original positions within the virtual exhibition.

This allowed physical work to remain part of the archived show rather than being reduced to flat reference images.

Matched views show how the original displays, scale and placement were carried into the explorable Fortnite environment.

From Blender to an interactive exhibition

The completed Blender environment was adapted for Unreal Editor for Fortnite so the archive could become an interactive space rather than only a collection of rendered images. Geometry, materials and lighting were prepared for real-time performance before the exhibition was rebuilt and published as a playable Fortnite island. 

Workbench

Visitors can move freely through the room, approach individual pieces and experience the scale and arrangement of the original show. Publishing through Fortnite also provides a familiar route into the project across supported platforms, while higher-end hardware can present the environment with its richest lighting and reflections. 

Outcome

The finished virtual exhibition extends a temporary event into a permanent, cross-platform experience. It preserves the identity, student work and spatial relationships of the Class of 2024 showcase as one explorable record rather than separating them across individual photographs and project files.

Publishing the environment through Fortnite made it accessible beyond a specialist 3D presentation: previous visitors can revisit the show, people who could not attend can experience its layout and the exhibition remains available after the physical installation has disappeared.
