Overview

Developed as part of GadJet's Product Design and Graphics team, the Display Visualiser is an interactive 3D retail-display experience for B2B customers. It allows display units to be inspected online and previewed at accurate scale within the customer's own store using mobile augmented reality before an order is placed.

The project connected two areas of work: defining a straightforward model-viewer experience and establishing a repeatable process for producing highly optimised, web-ready display assets.

The problem

B2B customers previously had to judge a display from product dimensions and static photographs. These communicate its appearance, but not how it will occupy a real retail environment. This is particularly important across compact counter displays, modular units and larger floor-standing displays, where footprint, height, visibility and surrounding space influence the purchasing decision.

The solution

The visualiser turns those measurements into an experience customers can inspect spatially. Each display can be rotated and examined as an interactive 3D model in the browser, then placed on the shop floor through mobile AR at its correct real-world scale. This gives retailers a clearer way to assess a stand before committing to it.

Building the display models

Eleven retail displays were prepared for the experience: five GadJet stands and six Goodiz stands. Each was recreated as a dedicated 3D asset with its overall dimensions and proportions kept accurate. Structural details, stocked products and branding were retained where they contributed to recognising and assessing the display.

Accuracy was especially important for augmented reality. A model that looks convincing but uses the wrong dimensions would give customers a misleading impression of the space required in their store.

Optimising for the web

The display models needed to preserve their visual character while loading and responding smoothly inside a browser. The number of textures was kept deliberately low and the web versions use lower-resolution WebP texture maps to reduce the delivered file size.

Repeated products and structural components were linked within each model so the same geometry could be reused across the stand instead of being stored repeatedly. This was particularly useful for stocked displays containing many instances of the same product.

Separate augmented-reality assets were prepared for Android and iOS, allowing customers to move closer to a display while retaining clear product and branding details. Android assets use higher-resolution WebP textures, while iOS assets use higher-resolution PNG and JPEG textures. Material properties were also tuned separately for the web viewer, Android and iOS so finishes remain convincing within the different rendering environments.

Lighting was ray traced and baked into the stand assets so the optimised web and AR models retain consistent light, shadow and material definition without requiring real-time ray tracing on the customer's device.

Together, these decisions keep relatively detailed retail displays practical across desktop and mobile hardware while allowing the AR versions to withstand closer inspection.

Interactive 3D viewer

On desktop, customers can rotate, zoom and pan around each display while viewing its dimensions, capacity and product information. Touch controls provide the same inspection workflow on mobile without requiring specialist software or a separate download.

The viewer keeps the model and practical specification together, allowing visual assessment and product comparison to happen in one place. The work is published across three live catalogue surfaces: the GadJet catalogue, the Goodiz catalogue and the GadJet Displays hub, which brings the AR-enabled stands from both brands together.

Accurate-scale augmented reality

The browser-based viewer supports the main modern desktop and mobile browsers. On relatively recent Android and iOS devices with native AR support, the selected stand can be opened in augmented reality and positioned directly on the shop floor. Because the model is prepared at accurate scale, customers can judge its footprint, height, visibility and relationship to surrounding fixtures before it arrives.

The experience turns the 3D catalogue into a practical planning tool: instead of imagining a stand from measurements alone, retailers can preview it within the environment where it will actually be used. Reflective material details were retained so the glossy and foil-finished graphics continue to respond as the customer moves around the model.

Placing the augmented-reality model beside the corresponding physical stand also provided a direct check of its proportions and real-world scale.

Outcome

The finished system gives B2B customers a consistent way to explore eleven counter, modular and floor-standing display options across GadJet and Goodiz before ordering. It combines product information, interactive 3D and accurate-scale mobile AR, helping retailers judge both the design and physical fit of a stand within their own store.

Rather than using 3D solely as presentation imagery, the visualiser turns the display assets into functional decision-making tools. The shared modelling, optimisation, lighting and platform-preparation process also provides a repeatable route for adding future stands while maintaining consistent quality across desktop, Android and iOS.
