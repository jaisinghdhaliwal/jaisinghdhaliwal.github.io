Material & depth

Glass, light and shadow connect the identity to Jai’s interest in reflections and rendering. They should make hierarchy tangible, not make every surface compete.

On this page Principle Glass Elevation Corners Application Misuse

01 / Principle

Depth with restraint.

Material effects communicate layering: a caption rests above a project, navigation floats above a page and a CV panel groups secondary information. If an effect does not explain that relationship, remove it.

01

One dominant raised layer

A view should have a clear surface hierarchy. Do not stack several equally blurred panels inside one another.

02

Light edges, dark shadows

Near-white borders describe the lit edge. A low-opacity tint of the primary dark creates the shadow and keeps depth connected to the palette.

03

Content remains opaque enough

Blur may soften what sits behind a panel, but primary-dark text must stay crisp and readable on every image.

02 / Glass

A functional frosted surface.

The standard glass recipe is warm, almost neutral and only slightly translucent. It is not a glossy decorative object.

Standard glass recipe

Surface

rgba(248, 248, 246, .76)

Use warm near-white so the panel belongs on the paper background.

Optics

blur(24px) saturate(1.25)

Blur establishes separation. Mild saturation prevents the content beneath from becoming muddy.

Lit edge

1px solid rgba(255, 255, 255, .88)

The border should be visible as a highlight, not as a grey outline.

Content

#141A28

Use the primary dark for all text and icons. Avoid translucent or coral body copy over imagery.

Use forNavigation, project captions and compact secondary panels.
Avoid forLong article backgrounds, every section on a page, or effects baked into the master logo.
FallbackWhen backdrop blur is unavailable, retain an opaque warm-white surface, border and shadow.

03 / Elevation

Three levels are enough.

Use elevation consistently so people can infer which surfaces are structural, interactive or temporarily prominent.

Level 0

Resting

Border only. Use for static grouping and cards that are not interactive.

Level 1

Raised

Use for glass navigation, captions and sidebar panels.

Level 2

Focused

Use temporarily for a hovered project or the most important floating control.

Level 01px structural line with no shadow.
Level 10 8px 24px rgba(17,29,56,.14), plus a subtle inset hairline.
Level 20 18px 38px rgba(15,22,37,.14) and 0 3px 8px rgba(15,22,37,.12).
Project focus0 30px 58px rgba(15,22,37,.24) and 0 9px 18px rgba(15,22,37,.16). Use only during hover or keyboard focus.

04 / Corners

Soft geometry, not bubbles.

Rounded corners connect interface controls with the continuous geometry of the J/SD mark. The radius follows the size and purpose of the surface.

Small / 10–13pxCompact controls, project captions and close-fitting panels.
Medium / 18–20pxProject cards, CV panels and standard content cards.
Large / 22–28pxFooter and large glass surfaces.
Pill / 999pxNavigation, segmented controls, status and compact actions only.
MobileReduce the base project radius from 18px to 14px so cards do not feel inflated on narrow screens.

Let the work demonstrate material

Reflections, ray-traced light and rendered surfaces belong in project imagery. Interface effects should support those interests rather than imitate them everywhere.

06 / Misuse

Keep glass believable.

Do

Use one clear panel, a light border, primary-dark copy and a shadow proportional to its elevation.

Avoid

Lens distortions, thick glowing borders, rainbow reflections, excessive transparency and multiple floating blobs.

Do

Increase shadow and scale together on interactive project cards, with a smooth transition.

Avoid

Applying hover growth to touch navigation or using motion that shifts surrounding layout.

Do

Keep the supplied logo artwork flat and unchanged.

Avoid

Baking glass, gradients, reflections or shadows into logo artwork.
