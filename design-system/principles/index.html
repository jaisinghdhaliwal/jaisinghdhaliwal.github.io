Design/Principles

Principles.

The identity is not defined by one logo or effect. These principles guide every decision across screen, print, motion and space.

On this page Clear first Work takes focus Depth with restraint Motion explains Every format Decision test

01 / Clear before clever

Understanding comes first.

Personality should make information more memorable, never more difficult to use. Headings say what a page contains, controls communicate their state and visual effects preserve legibility.

01

Use familiar language

Prefer “Download CV” and “View work” to abstract or overly branded action labels.

02

Build visible hierarchy

Every screen should have an obvious entry point, supporting context and next action.

Do

Use one strong headline, a concise introduction and concrete navigation labels.

Avoid

Making viewers decode a visual metaphor before they can reach the work.

02 / Work takes focus

The system frames the work.

The portfolio contains branding, photography, 3D renders and moving images with very different visual languages. The surrounding interface remains calm so that this variety feels intentional rather than chaotic.

03 / Depth with restraint

Depth must have a job.

Frosted glass, thin highlights and primary-dark shadows connect the identity to Jai’s interest in rendering, light and reflection. They establish layers and interaction—not spectacle.

01

One clear raised layer

A project card may contain one frosted caption surface. Do not stack multiple translucent panels over one another.

02

Keep the logo flat

Material expression belongs to applications. Use the supplied landscape, square or full-name SVG without adding effects.

04 / Motion explains

Movement reveals continuity.

The selected navigation pill travels between pages, cards appear when they enter view and comparison controls expose process. Motion should clarify where something came from or what changed.

Do

Animate state changes, spatial relationships and sequential media.

Avoid

Looping decorative animation that competes with project videos or ignores reduced-motion preferences.

05 / Built for every format

Same identity, equivalent experience.

Desktop, tablet, mobile and print do not need identical layouts. They must retain the same content, priority and character. No essential information may depend on hover, HDR or a large screen.

DesktopBroad grids, sticky documentation navigation and pointer-enhanced interaction.
MobileSingle-column reading, reachable controls and permanently visible project information.
PrintApproved logo artwork, restrained ink coverage and no dependence on transparency.
Motion reducedInstant state changes and static poster imagery preserve all information.

Decision test

Before adding anything, ask five questions.

  1. Does this make the content clearer?
  2. Does it support rather than compete with the work?
  3. Does its depth or motion explain hierarchy or state?
  4. Does it remain usable without hover, animation or transparency?
  5. Will it still feel recognisably J/SD in print and at small sizes?
