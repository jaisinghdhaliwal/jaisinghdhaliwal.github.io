Voice & content

Write like a thoughtful designer showing the work directly: warm, specific and curious, with enough technical detail to be credible and no unnecessary studio language.

On this page Voice Positioning Site language Project writing Writing style Accessible content

01 / Voice

Clear, personal and grounded.

The voice belongs to an individual designer, not an anonymous agency. Use first person, explain decisions plainly and let the breadth of the work demonstrate ambition.

01

Warm

Invite people in with direct language such as “Hello, I’m Jai.” Avoid a distant biography in the third person.

02

Specific

Name the output, role, tools or interaction. “A responsive degree-show website built in HTML, CSS and JavaScript” is stronger than “an innovative digital solution.”

03

Curious

Use capture, reconstruct, render and imagine to express an interest in how images, objects and spaces are made.

04

Confident, not inflated

State responsibilities and outcomes honestly. Avoid superlatives that the project cannot demonstrate.

04 / Project writing

Lead with what was made.

Project pages should move from the visible result to role, decisions, process and outcome. Keep the opening summary short enough to sit beside strong imagery.

01

What

Name the project and the form it takes.

02

Why

Describe the audience, need or question in one clear sentence.

03

Role

State personal responsibility precisely, especially where a team contributed.

04

How

Explain the most important design or technical decision rather than listing every tool.

05

Result

Close with a concrete outcome, working prototype, published link or lesson.

Example / Just Design

Just Design - virtual exhibition

A virtual university exhibition presenting student graphic design work in a navigable rendered environment. I developed the scene, visual system and digital experience as an extension of the degree-show project.

05 / Writing style

Keep hierarchy consistent in words too.

Page titlesUse sentence or title case: “Create”, “Jai Singh Dhaliwal”, “Material & depth”. Do not set large headings in all capitals.
Interface labelsFira Code labels may be visually uppercase in CSS. Keep source copy short and plain.
Brand nameUse “Jai Singh Dhaliwal” in formal contexts, “Jai” conversationally and J/SD only for the mark or compact signature.
Project namesPreserve official spelling: “GadJet Display Visualiser”, “GadJet Packaging”, “JustDesign.Show” and “Just Design Virtual Exhibition”.
PunctuationUse a normal hyphen to separate a title from a descriptor. Avoid repeated slashes outside the visual identity system.
NumbersUse four-digit years and keep date ranges consistent.
Do

“I designed packaging, product manuals, retail displays, marketing emails and 3D product renders.”

Avoid

“I deliver cutting-edge, multidisciplinary creative solutions across a wide range of exciting touchpoints.”

06 / Accessible content

Clarity is part of the identity.

LinksDescribe the destination or action. Do not rely on “here,” colour or an icon alone.
Alternative textDescribe the subject and useful visible detail: “GadJet retail display shown at accurate scale in augmented reality,” not “Project image.”
Decorative mediaUse empty alt text for repeated or purely atmospheric images so assistive technology can skip them.
VideoProvide equivalent surrounding copy and a representative poster. Add captions when speech or meaningful audio is introduced.
InstructionsUse input-neutral language when possible. Prefer “Select a country” over “Click a country.”
ContactWrite the email address and phone number as visible text as well as functional links.
